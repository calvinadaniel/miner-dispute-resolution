The arbitration process

A formal dispute resolution path outside the traditional court system — with a neutral decision-maker and a final award.

Why arbitration

Streamlined. Controlled. Decisive.

In arbitration, parties voluntarily submit their dispute to a neutral arbitrator who acts as a private judge — carefully reviewing evidence and arguments from both sides.

Compared with litigation, arbitration often offers a more efficient process, greater control over selection of the decision-maker, and a more timely, cost-conscious resolution.

How it works

Three movements to a final decision

Selection of an arbitrator

Parties must mutually agree on an arbitrator and equally share the cost. They agree that the arbitrator’s decision is final.

Process management

The parties design and implement the process. The arbitrator manages it — making fair decisions when the parties reach an impasse before or during the hearing.

A final decision

The arbitrator issues a final decision based on the facts presented, free of bias, and bound by the highest standards of ethics and integrity.
