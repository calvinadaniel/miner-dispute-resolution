The mediation process

A voluntary, collaborative path where disputing parties keep maximum control over both the process and its outcome.

Why mediation

Dialogue first. Agreement by the parties.

Unlike litigation — where a judge or jury imposes a decision — mediation empowers parties to participate in crafting a solution that addresses their specific needs and concerns.

The mediator facilitates communication, identifies common ground, and helps explore creative options. The dispute cannot be resolved by the mediator alone; resolution belongs to the parties.

How it works

From positions to shared interests

A mediator is selected

Mediation involves a neutral — someone with no interest in the dispute and no conflicting ties to either party.

The process begins

The mediator helps parties move from opening positions toward shared interests by defining the dispute, asking questions, and developing options.

Agreement by the parties

Only the parties can resolve the conflict — by reaching an agreement. The mediator guides; the parties decide.
